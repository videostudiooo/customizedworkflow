# Editorial planning

## Brief

Capture only what changes the edit:

- goal: what the viewer should understand, feel or do;
- audience and context;
- delivery platform and aspect ratio;
- target duration or an explicit assumption;
- tone, pacing and visual references;
- source media and usage rights;
- voice, music and caption needs.

If details are missing, choose safe explicit assumptions and continue. Ask at most three questions before a complex first build.

## Plan by complexity

For a simple change, state the intended outcome in one line and execute.

For a guided production, write a compact plan in `notes.md` with:

- thesis and hook;
- one sentence per beat;
- source/select for each beat;
- intended duration or frame range;
- picture, text, voice, music and SFX cues;
- planned transition only when it has narrative meaning;
- review frames;
- risks requiring user judgment.

Use frames as the timing source of truth:

```text
frames = round(seconds × fps)
seconds = frames ÷ fps
```

## Editorial rules

- Open with meaning or the result, not an empty logo animation.
- Let authentic source evidence provide the argument.
- Vary shot duration and density when the content changes; avoid identical scene lengths.
- Use a transition only when it communicates a change in time, place or idea.
- Keep one focal point per frame.
- Give text enough reading time: usually at least 1.5 seconds, longer for names and instructions.
- Keep important UI, faces and captions out of platform-obscured safe zones.
- Preserve original dialogue unless replacement was requested.
- Do not copy a reference film's exact style; extract reusable principles.

## Review loop

1. Assemble for story and sync.
2. Review representative frames and a low-resolution draft.
3. Fix structural problems before polish.
4. Review both wide and mobile.
5. Render the master only after picture, text, audio and timing are stable.
